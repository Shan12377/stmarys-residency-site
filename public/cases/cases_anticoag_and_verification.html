Practice Cases: Anticoagulation and Order Verification

All cases are invented for teaching. Built from the clinical interview questions, with updated numbers. 60 seconds each, out loud. Top 10% = answer + source and year + exact order + monitoring. Key: ✅✅ two independent sources · ✅ one source · ⚠️ [VERIFY]

Study pages: Anticoagulation_Core · Heparin · Enoxaparin · 4F_PCC · Insulin_IV_DKA · Potassium_Chloride_IV

Case 1 (Anticoag + surgery): Apixaban before a hip replacement

An 82 year old, 58 kg, SCr 1.1, takes apixaban 5 mg BID for atrial fibrillation. Elective hip replacement in 4 days. The surgeon asks when to stop it and whether to bridge.

Model answer: “Two catches. First, she meets 2 of 3 dose reduction criteria (age 80 or older, weight 60 kg or less), so her AF dose should be 2.5 mg BID. Second, a hip replacement is high bleed risk, so per PAUSE she skips 2 days before surgery and the day of surgery: last dose 3 days before. No bridging for a DOAC. Restart 48 to 72 hours after surgery once bleeding is controlled (CHEST 2022).” Source check: dose reduction ✅✅ (Eliquis label, SmPC); PAUSE schedule ✅ (WikiJournalClub); CHEST 2022 resume timing ✅. See Anticoagulation_Core sections 1 and 3.

Case 2 (Warfarin): INR 7.2, then a bleed

A 70 kg man on warfarin for AF has an INR of 7.2 and no bleeding. The resident orders vitamin K 10 mg IV. Two days later he has a GI bleed, INR 5.1.

Model answer: “Day 1: hold warfarin and recheck the INR. With INR 4.5 to 10 and no bleeding, vitamin K isn’t needed (Crowther, Ann Intern Med 2009), and 10 mg IV is too much: it risks warfarin resistance when we restart. Day 2, major bleed: vitamin K 10 mg IV slow infusion plus 4F-PCC. INR 4 to 6 is 35 units/kg, so 2,450 units, under the 3,500 max. In the label trials the INR was checked 30 minutes after the infusion; repeat PCC dosing is not recommended.” Source check: INR 4.5 to 10 ✅✅ (UMEM on Crowther; Sunnybrook); PCC dosing ✅✅ (4F_PCC); 30 minute INR and no repeat dosing ✅ Kcentra prescribing information (Drugs.com).

Case 3 (HIT): Day 7 on heparin, kidneys failing

A patient on a heparin drip for 7 days: platelets fell from 240 K to 95 K. New thrombosis in the left arm. CrCl 18. A heparin flush order is pending.

Model answer: “This looks like HIT: more than a 50% drop, 5 to 10 days in, plus a new clot. Reject the flush and stop all heparin, including flushes and enoxaparin. Send a PF4 antibody test. With CrCl 18, argatroban fits because it’s cleared by the liver; it raises the INR, which matters when we switch to warfarin. No warfarin until platelets recover, and no routine platelet transfusion. Add heparin to the allergy list.” Source check: see Heparin HIT section ✅; warfarin after platelets reach 150,000 ✅ The Blood Project: switching off argatroban in HIT (ASH 2018).

Case 4 (Anticoag + L&D): Epidural on enoxaparin

A pregnant patient on enoxaparin 40 mg SC daily got her last dose at 0600. Anesthesia wants to place an epidural at 1400.

Model answer: “Too early. Prophylactic enoxaparin needs 12 hours before neuraxial placement, so not before 1800. If she were on a treatment dose, 24 hours. After the catheter comes out, wait at least 4 hours for the next dose. I’d call anesthesia and the OB team now so they can plan.” Source check: ✅✅ (Enoxaparin label; ASRA via NYSORA). See Enoxaparin and Anticoagulation_Core section 4.

Case 5 (ID): C. diff on IV vancomycin

C. diff positive. Order: vancomycin 125 mg IV q6h.

Model answer: “Wrong route. IV vancomycin doesn’t reach the colon. Per IDSA 2021, fidaxomicin 200 mg PO BID for 10 days is preferred; vancomycin 125 mg PO QID for 10 days is an alternative. Call the prescriber and change it.” Source check: ✅✅ (AAFP summary of IDSA 2021; UIC Drug Information Group).

Case 6 (ID + psych): Linezolid and sertraline

A VRE bacteremia patient on linezolid has her home sertraline 100 mg resumed.

Model answer: “Flag serotonin syndrome. The FDA warns about linezolid with serotonergic drugs. I’d call the team: weigh holding sertraline (it has a long washout) or an alternative such as daptomycin if it fits the infection, and if they continue both, monitor for agitation, fever, tremor and clonus. Document.” Source check: ✅ (FDA safety communication).

Case 7 (Interaction): Ciprofloxacin with binders

Ciprofloxacin 500 mg PO BID; the patient also takes calcium carbonate TID and sevelamer.

Model answer: “Chelation cuts ciprofloxacin absorption. Give ciprofloxacin at least 2 hours before or 6 hours after calcium and binders. Also check the renal dose and other QT drugs.” Source check: ✅ (ciprofloxacin label on DailyMed).

Case 8 (Renal): Morphine on hemodialysis

Order: morphine 4 mg IV q2h PRN pain, patient on hemodialysis.

Model answer: “Morphine’s active metabolites build up in kidney failure and cause sedation and neurotoxicity. Recommend a low dose of hydromorphone or fentanyl, and monitor sedation and breathing.” Source check: ⚠️ [VERIFY with a renal dosing reference].

Case 9 (Safety): Insulin for hyperkalemia

Order: “Insulin regular 10U IV x1” for K 6.8 with peaked T waves. No dextrose ordered. CrCl 12.

Model answer: “Three things. Calcium gluconate 1 g IV first to stabilize the heart. ‘U’ is an ISMP Do Not Use abbreviation (read as 0 or 4), so rewrite as ‘10 units.’ And insulin needs dextrose 25 g; with CrCl 12, consider 5 units to lower hypoglycemia risk, and check glucose hourly for several hours.” Source check: Do Not Use list ✅ (ISMP); 5 unit option ✅ (see Adult_Critical_Care section 3). See Insulin_IV_DKA.

Case 10 (Oncology safety): Methotrexate daily, vincristine in a syringe

Two orders on one patient: methotrexate 7.5 mg PO daily for rheumatoid arthritis (home med), and vincristine 2 mg prepared in a 10 mL syringe while intrathecal methotrexate is also scheduled.

Model answer: “Both are classic fatal errors. RA methotrexate is weekly: confirm with the patient and outpatient pharmacy, then correct to once weekly on a named day. Vincristine goes in a minibag, never a syringe, labeled ‘For intravenous use only. Fatal if given by other routes,’ and stays apart from intrathecal doses.” Source check: ✅✅ (ISMP vincristine labeling; WHO alert 115); weekly methotrexate ✅ (ISMP Targeted Best Practices).

Note: the raw interview prep DKA case uses a potassium cutoff of 3.3 for adults. The 2024 adult consensus uses 3.5; see Insulin_IV_DKA.
