# Journal Club Template (trial to bedside in 60 seconds)

Use this for every trial. Fill the **Bottom line** last but say it first. Built from the JAMA Users' Guides to the Medical Literature (three questions: Are the results valid? What are the results? Will they help my patients?), the Cochrane Risk of Bias 2 domains, the CONSORT 2025 reporting checklist (Hopewell et al., BMJ 2025;389:e081123), and standard pharmacy residency journal club evaluation forms.

---

## 0. The 60 second bottom line (say this first)
> "[Trial name] ([journal, year]) randomized [N] [population] to [intervention] vs [comparator] for [duration]. The primary outcome, [outcome], occurred in [x%] vs [y%] (ARR [z%], NNT [n]; HR [ ], 95% CI [ ]). The main limitation is [one]. For our patients at St. Mary's, this means [practice implication]."

## 1. Citation and context
- Citation, trial name, funding source, authors' conflicts of interest.
- Why was this trial done? What was standard care before? What gap does it fill?
- Registered (ClinicalTrials.gov number)? Was the primary outcome the same as registered?

## 2. PICO
| | |
|---|---|
| **P**opulation | Key inclusion and exclusion criteria; who was left out (pregnant, renal impairment, elderly, peds) |
| **I**ntervention | Drug, dose, route, duration, titration |
| **C**omparator | Placebo or active? Was the comparator dosed fairly (standard of care, right dose)? |
| **O**utcomes | Primary; key secondary; safety |
| Design | RCT, superiority vs noninferiority vs equivalence, blinding, sites, duration |

## 3. Are the results valid? (internal validity)
| Question | What to look for | Red flag |
|---|---|---|
| Randomized properly? | Method (computer, central) | Alternating days, chart numbers |
| Allocation concealed? | Central or sealed opaque envelopes | Investigators could predict the next assignment |
| Groups similar at baseline? | Table 1 | Big differences in severity or key risk factors |
| Blinded? | Patients, clinicians, outcome assessors | Open label with a subjective outcome |
| Analyzed as randomized? | Intention to treat (ITT) for superiority | Only per protocol, or a "modified ITT" that drops many patients |
| Follow up complete? | Loss to follow up and why | Losses larger than the number of events, or unequal between groups |
| Stopped early for benefit? | Planned interim analyses | Early stopping inflates effect size |
| Powered? | Sample size calculation and assumed event rate | Event rate much lower than assumed: underpowered |
| Primary outcome meaningful? | Patient oriented (death, stroke, MI) vs surrogate (A1c, LDL, INR) | Surrogate only; composite driven by its softest part |
| Noninferiority trial? | Margin stated and justified; both ITT and per protocol analyzed | Wide margin; only ITT reported (ITT biases toward noninferiority) |
| Multiple comparisons? | Prespecified secondaries and subgroups | Many subgroups, positive one highlighted |

## 4. What are the results?
- **Primary outcome:** events in each arm, effect size, 95% CI, p value.
- **Absolute risk reduction (ARR)** = control event rate minus treatment event rate.
- **Relative risk reduction (RRR)** = ARR ÷ control event rate.
- **NNT** = 1 ÷ ARR (round up). **NNH** = 1 ÷ absolute risk increase for harm.
- **Hazard ratio / odds ratio / relative risk:** below 1 favors treatment (for bad outcomes). If the 95% CI crosses 1, it is not statistically significant.
- **Statistical vs clinical significance:** a significant p value with a tiny ARR may not change practice.
- **Fragility index** (optional): how many patients would need a different outcome to lose significance. A small number means a fragile result.
- **Secondary outcomes** are hypothesis generating if the primary is negative.
- **Safety:** serious adverse events, discontinuations, deaths; who paid for the safety monitoring.

## 5. Will it help my patients? (external validity)
- Would my patient have been enrolled? (age, kidney function, comorbidities, pregnancy, weight)
- Is the comparator what we use at St. Mary's?
- Is the dose, monitoring or setting realistic here? (labs, pumps, nursing time)
- Formulary, cost, availability, shortages.
- What do current guidelines say, and does this trial change them?

- Do the data support the authors' wording?
- **Your role in therapy statement:** "For [patient type], [drug] is a reasonable [first line / alternative / not recommended] because [reason]."
- What further research is needed?

## 8. Pharmacist's angle (always add)
- Dosing, renal and hepatic adjustment, interactions, monitoring, administration.
- Formulary or protocol change this could support at St. Mary's.

## Formula card
| | |
|---|---|
| ARR | CER − EER |
| RRR | ARR ÷ CER |
| NNT | 1 ÷ ARR |
| Relative risk | EER ÷ CER |
| 95% CI of a ratio crossing 1 | Not significant |
| 95% CI of a difference crossing 0 | Not significant |

CER = control event rate; EER = experimental event rate.

## How you'll be scored (residency style)
1. Accurate summary of design, methods and results.
2. Critique: strengths, weaknesses and their impact on the result.
3. Conclusion with a role in therapy and limitations.
4. Background knowledge: disease state and the drug's pharmacology.
5. Delivery: on time, clear, answers questions.
