# Journal Club: ESETT (second line drugs for status epilepticus)

**Key:** ✅✅ two independent sources · ✅ one source · ⚠️ [VERIFY] · Template: [[Journal_Club_Template]] · Module: [[PICU_Core]] · [[Codes_Resuscitation]]

## 0. 60 second bottom line
> "ESETT (NEJM 2019) randomized children and adults with benzodiazepine refractory convulsive status epilepticus to levetiracetam 60 mg/kg, fosphenytoin 20 mg PE/kg, or valproate 40 mg/kg over 10 minutes. Seizures stopped with improved responsiveness at 60 minutes in 47%, 45% and 46%: no meaningful difference. Safety was similar, with numerically more hypotension and intubation with fosphenytoin. The main limitation is early stopping and unblinding. For our ED and PICU, any of the three is acceptable second line, so pick by patient factors." ✅✅ [1][2]

## 1. Citation and context
Kapur J, et al. N Engl J Med 2019;381(22):2103-2113 ✅ [PubMed 31774955](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31774955/). NIH funded ✅ [1]. Before ESETT, second line choice had little head to head evidence.

## 2. PICO
| | |
|---|---|
| Population | Age 2 and older with convulsive status epilepticus despite adequate benzodiazepines; 57 US EDs ✅ [1] |
| Intervention and comparators (all over 10 min) | Levetiracetam 60 mg/kg (max 4,500 mg); fosphenytoin 20 mg PE/kg (max 1,500 mg PE); valproate 40 mg/kg (max 3,000 mg) ✅✅ [1][2] |
| Outcome | Seizure cessation and improved responsiveness at 60 min without more anticonvulsants |
| Design | Blinded, response adaptive, stopped early for futility (384 analyzed in the three arms) ✅✅ [1][2] |

## 3. Validity
- Strengths: blinded, head to head, pragmatic ED setting.
- Red flags: stopped early; 23% to 33% per group did not meet eligibility; unblinding in about half of enrollments; mixed children and adults; responsiveness is subjective ✅✅ [1][2].

## 4. Results
| Outcome | Levetiracetam | Fosphenytoin | Valproate | Check |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Primary success | 47% | 45% | 46% | ✅✅ [1][2] |
| Probability of being best | 0.41 | 0.24 | 0.35 | ✅ [2] |
| Life threatening hypotension | 0.7% | 3.2% | 1.6% | ✅✅ [1][2] |
| Intubation within 60 min | 20% | 26% | 17% | ✅✅ [1][2] |
| Death | 4.7% | 2.4% | 1.6% | ✅✅ [1][2] |

## 5. Applicability at St. Mary's
Children's hospital and adult ED both apply (age 2 and up). Choose by patient: valproate avoided in pregnancy, liver disease and suspected mitochondrial disease; fosphenytoin needs cardiac monitoring; levetiracetam needs renal adjustment for maintenance.

## 6. Role in therapy
"For benzodiazepine refractory status epilepticus, levetiracetam, fosphenytoin or valproate are equally effective second line options; choose by patient factors."

## 7. Pharmacist's angle
- Check weight and the max dose for each drug.
- Fosphenytoin is dosed in PE; max rate 150 mg PE/min in adults, and in children 2 mg PE/kg/min or 150 mg PE/min, whichever is slower; monitor ECG and BP during and 10 to 20 min after ✅ [Fosphenytoin label (Hikma, via Janusinfo)](https://janusinfo.se/download/18.3a466c131894862aa4257a2e/1689331426425/Fosphenytoin%20sodium.pdf).
- Valproate: pregnancy, liver, and age under 2 risks.
- Have the third line (anesthetic infusion) ready.
