# Enoxaparin (Lovenox) · Drug Monograph

*Drug Monographs section (does the job of a general drug reference such as Lexicomp or Micromedex). Built from public sources. Confirm against St. Mary's protocol and the hospital's drug reference before clinical use.*

**Verification key:** ✅✅ confirmed in two independent sources · ✅ one source · ⚠️ [VERIFY] not yet confirmed twice. Two versions of the same FDA label count as one source.

ISMP High Alert Medication (anticoagulant). **Boxed warning: spinal and epidural hematoma** with neuraxial anesthesia or spinal puncture, which can cause permanent paralysis ✅✅ [1][2].

---

## Adult dosing (SC unless noted)
| Indication | Dose | CrCl below 30 mL/min | Check | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Medical VTE prophylaxis | 40 mg once daily | 30 mg once daily | ✅✅ | [1][2] |
| Abdominal surgery prophylaxis | 40 mg once daily, first dose 2 h before surgery | 30 mg once daily | ✅ | [1] |
| Hip or knee replacement | 30 mg every 12 h starting 12 to 24 h after surgery (hip alternative: 40 mg daily) | 30 mg once daily | ✅ | [1] |
| DVT or PE treatment | 1 mg/kg every 12 h, or 1.5 mg/kg once daily (inpatient) | 1 mg/kg once daily | ✅✅ | [1][2] |
| Unstable angina / NSTEMI | 1 mg/kg every 12 h with aspirin | 1 mg/kg once daily | ✅✅ | [1][2] |
| STEMI, under 75 years | 30 mg IV bolus + 1 mg/kg SC, then 1 mg/kg every 12 h (max 100 mg for the first 2 doses) | 30 mg IV bolus + 1 mg/kg, then 1 mg/kg once daily | ✅✅ | [1][2] |
| STEMI, 75 years or older | **No IV bolus.** 0.75 mg/kg every 12 h (max 75 mg for the first 2 doses) | 1 mg/kg once daily, no bolus | ✅✅ | [1][2] |
| STEMI going to PCI | Last SC dose under 8 h before balloon: nothing extra. Over 8 h: 0.3 mg/kg IV bolus | | ✅ | [1] |

- CrCl 30 to 80 mL/min: no dose change, but watch closely for bleeding ✅ [1].
- **Low body weight** (women under 45 kg, men under 57 kg): higher exposure on fixed prophylaxis doses; watch for bleeding ✅ [1].
- **Obesity (BMI over 30):** no consensus on prophylaxis dose adjustment per the label ✅ [1]. Know St. Mary's obesity protocol ⚠️ [VERIFY].

## Monitoring
- PT and aPTT do **not** measure enoxaparin effect ✅ [1].
- Anti Xa is used in renal impairment, obesity, pregnancy, children or bleeding ✅✅ [1][2].
- **Anti Xa targets (treatment):** draw a peak about 4 hours after the dose, after the 3rd dose (steady state) ✅✅ [2][3].
  - 1 mg/kg every 12 h: peak 0.5 to 1.0 units/mL ✅ [3]
  - 1.5 mg/kg once daily: peak 1.0 to 2.0 units/mL ✅ [3]
  - Confirm St. Mary's lab ranges ⚠️ [VERIFY]
- Platelets (HIT risk is lower than with heparin but present), hemoglobin, SCr.

## Neuraxial timing (epidural, spinal, catheter)
- Prophylactic dose: wait **at least 12 hours** after the dose before placement or removal ✅✅ [2][4].
- Treatment dose (1 mg/kg every 12 h or 1.5 mg/kg daily): wait **at least 24 hours** ✅✅ [2][4].
- After catheter removal: next dose no sooner than 4 hours later ✅ [2].
- ASRA updated its guideline after the 2010 edition cited here; check the current edition and St. Mary's anesthesia policy ⚠️ [VERIFY].

## Reversal (protamine)
- Enoxaparin given within 8 hours: **1 mg protamine per 1 mg enoxaparin** ✅✅ [1][2].
- More than 8 hours, or a second dose needed: 0.5 mg per 1 mg ✅ [1].
- 12 hours or more since the dose: protamine may not be needed ✅ [1].
- Protamine reverses only about 60% of anti Xa activity ✅✅ [1][2].
- Max 50 mg per dose ✅ [2]. Risk of hypotension and anaphylactoid reactions; give slowly with resuscitation available ✅ [1].

## Contraindications
Active major bleeding; immune mediated HIT within the past 100 days or circulating antibodies; hypersensitivity to enoxaparin, heparin or pork products; benzyl alcohol allergy (multiple dose vials) ✅ [1].

## Pediatrics and neonates
Not FDA approved for neonates or infants, though it is used off label with anti Xa monitoring. Multiple dose vials contain **benzyl alcohol** (gasping syndrome); use single dose syringes ✅ [1]. Pediatric dosing ⚠️ [VERIFY against the pediatric dosing reference St. Mary's uses].

## Pharmacist verification checklist
1. Indication matches the dose (prophylaxis vs treatment vs ACS).
2. **Weight** and **CrCl** (Cockcroft Gault) both current.
3. Age 75 or older with STEMI: no bolus, 0.75 mg/kg.
4. Epidural or spinal planned or in place? Check timing.
5. Heparin running or given recently? Avoid double anticoagulation.
6. Platelets, hemoglobin, anti Xa plan if renal, obese or pregnant.
