# Pregnancy and Lactation

Her own quick reference for L&D, postpartum and NICU mothers. **LactMed is the primary free source for breastfeeding** ([LactMed, NIH](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501922/)); the AAP calls it the most complete and current resource ✅ [1]. For pregnancy, read the label's section 8.1 on [DailyMed](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/); Briggs is available through St. Mary's.
**Key:** ✅✅ two independent sources · ✅ one source · ⚠️ [VERIFY]

---

## 1. How to answer "Is it safe?"
1. **Pregnancy:** the old A, B, C, D, X letters were **removed** by the FDA Pregnancy and Lactation Labeling Rule (effective June 30, 2015). Labels now have 8.1 Pregnancy (risk summary, clinical considerations, data, registry), 8.2 Lactation, and 8.3 Females and Males of Reproductive Potential ✅✅ [2][3]. Don't answer with a letter.
2. **Lactation:** look up the drug in LactMed. Use the **relative infant dose (RID)**: the infant's dose from milk as a percent of the mother's weight adjusted dose. **Under 10% is generally acceptable** ✅ [1].
3. Better choices in breastfeeding: short half life, high protein binding, low oral bioavailability, large molecule, RID under 10% ✅ [1].
4. Consider the infant: preterm and newborns clear drugs slowly.
5. Give a clear answer and a plan ("compatible; watch the baby for sleepiness"), not "consult your doctor."

## 2. Quick answers for L&D and postpartum
| Drug or class | Pregnancy | Breastfeeding | Check | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **NSAIDs** (ibuprofen, ketorolac) | **Avoid from 20 weeks** (fetal kidney injury, low amniotic fluid); if needed 20 to 30 weeks, lowest dose, shortest time, ultrasound if more than 48 h; avoid from 30 weeks (ductus closure). Low dose aspirin 81 mg is exempt | Ibuprofen is a preferred NSAID ⚠️ [VERIFY in LactMed] | ✅ | [4] |
| Acetaminophen | Option for pain and fever | **Good choice**; infant gets about 2% or less of the weight adjusted dose | ✅✅ | [4] · [LactMed: acetaminophen](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501194/) |
| **Codeine, tramadol** | | **Don't breastfeed** while taking them (ultra rapid CYP2D6 metabolism; infant deaths) | ✅ | [5] |
| Other opioids (oxycodone, hydrocodone, morphine) | | Oxycodone: LactMed limit about **60 mg/day for 2 to 3 days** with infants under 2 months (one hospital uses 40 mg/day max for 3 days); watch the infant for sleepiness, poor feeding and breathing pauses | ✅ | [Respiratory Therapy: oxycodone and breastfeeding (cites LactMed limits)](https://respiratory-therapy.com/public-health/pediatrics/neonatal/neonatal-respiratory-depression-maternal-oxycodone-breastfeeding/) |

| Methyldopa | Safe; second line | Compatible; second line | ✅ | [6] |

## 3. Pharmacist moves
- On L&D, flag any NSAID order at 20 weeks or later before delivery (indomethacin for preterm labor is a planned, time limited exception) ✅ [4] · [[LD_Obstetrics_Core]].
- Postpartum: if codeine or tramadol is ordered for a breastfeeding mother, recommend an alternative ✅ [5].
- Postpartum hypertension in a breastfeeding mother: labetalol, nifedipine ER, amlodipine or enalapril ✅ [6].
- Document the source you used (LactMed date) when you answer a nurse or mother.
