# PICU and Pediatric Emergencies: Pharmacist Core

**Pharmacist's job in the PICU:** weight based emergency doses without errors, safe standard concentration drips, sedation and withdrawal plans, and adult max doses as the ceiling.

Sources: AHA/AAP Pediatric Advanced Life Support guideline (2025, Part 8, Circulation 2025;152 suppl 2); Neurocritical Care Society and American Epilepsy Society status epilepticus guidelines (AES 2016; ESETT trial, NEJM 2019); ISPAD Clinical Practice Consensus Guidelines, DKA (2022); AAP Clinical Practice Guideline on maintenance IV fluids (2018); SCCM PANDEM guidelines for pediatric ICU pain, agitation, neuromuscular blockade, delirium and iatrogenic withdrawal (Pediatr Crit Care Med, 2022).

---

## 1. The pediatric rules
- Dose in mg/kg, but **never exceed the adult dose**. A 70 kg teenager gets an adult dose.
- Use the measured weight in kg. For obese children, know whether the drug uses actual or ideal weight.
- Standard concentrations matched to the smart pump library; no custom concentrations unless required.
- Check the volume can actually be measured (very small volumes need dilution).

## 2. PALS code doses (AHA 2025)
| Drug | Dose | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Epinephrine (0.1 mg/mL) | 0.01 mg/kg IV/IO (0.1 mL/kg), max 1 mg, every 3 to 5 minutes. ETT: 0.1 mg/kg | 2025: give as early as possible in nonshockable rhythms |
| Amiodarone | 5 mg/kg IV/IO bolus (max 300 mg), may repeat up to 15 mg/kg total | For shock refractory VF or pulseless VT |
| Lidocaine | 1 mg/kg IV/IO | Alternative to amiodarone |
| Adenosine | 0.1 mg/kg rapid push (max 6 mg); second dose 0.2 mg/kg (max 12 mg) | SVT; rapid flush, closest port |
| Atropine | 0.02 mg/kg IV (max single dose 0.5 mg) | Bradycardia from vagal tone or AV block |
| Calcium chloride 10% | 20 mg/kg (0.2 mL/kg) IV/IO, central preferred | Hyperkalemia, hypocalcemia, calcium channel blocker overdose |
| Calcium gluconate 10% | 60 mg/kg (0.6 mL/kg) IV | Peripheral friendly option [VERIFY dose in St. Mary's sheet] |
| Dextrose | 0.5 to 1 g/kg (D10W 5 to 10 mL/kg) | Hypoglycemia |
| Sodium bicarbonate | 1 mEq/kg | Not routine; hyperkalemia or tricyclic overdose |
| Defibrillation | 2 J/kg, then 4 J/kg, max 10 J/kg or adult dose | Not a drug, but you'll hear it |

**Quote it:** "PALS 2025 emphasizes epinephrine as early as possible in nonshockable arrest; the dose is 0.01 mg/kg of the 0.1 mg/mL, max 1 mg."

## 3. Status epilepticus
| Time | Step | Dose |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 5 min | Stabilize, check glucose | Dextrose if low |
| 5 to 20 min | Benzodiazepine | Lorazepam 0.1 mg/kg IV (max 4 mg), may repeat once; or midazolam IM 0.2 mg/kg (max 10 mg); or diazepam rectal |
| 20 to 40 min | Second line (ESETT: similar efficacy) | Levetiracetam 60 mg/kg IV (max 4,500 mg); or fosphenytoin 20 mg PE/kg (max 1,500 mg PE); or valproate 40 mg/kg (max 3,000 mg) |
| 40+ min | Refractory | Infusion (midazolam, pentobarbital, ketamine) with ICU and EEG |

Pharmacist watch: fosphenytoin is dosed in **PE (phenytoin equivalents)**; valproate is avoided in suspected mitochondrial or metabolic disease and in young children with unknown liver disease.

## 4. Pediatric DKA (ISPAD 2022)
- Fluids first: 10 to 20 mL/kg isotonic bolus.
- **Insulin 0.05 to 0.1 units/kg/h IV, no bolus**, started after the first hour of fluids.
- Potassium: if the child is hypokalemic, start potassium replacement before insulin (ISPAD 2022); many protocols hold insulin until K is at least 3.3 [VERIFY St. Mary's cutoff]. If K is normal, add it with insulin; if high, wait for urine output. **Adults are different:** the 2024 adult consensus holds insulin until K is above 3.5 (see [[Insulin_IV_DKA]]).
- Add dextrose when glucose falls to about 250 to 300 mg/dL (two bag system).
- **Cerebral edema** (headache, falling heart rate, rising BP, change in mental status): mannitol 0.5 to 1 g/kg over 10 to 15 min, or 3% saline 2.5 to 5 mL/kg. Have it available.

## 5. Rapid sequence intubation (pediatric)
| Drug | Dose |
|---|---|
| Ketamine | 1 to 2 mg/kg IV |
| Etomidate | 0.3 mg/kg IV (adrenal suppression; avoid in septic shock per many protocols) |
| Rocuronium | 1 to 1.2 mg/kg IV |
| Succinylcholine | 2 mg/kg in infants, 1 to 1.5 mg/kg in children (avoid in hyperkalemia, neuromuscular disease, burns or crush after 24 to 48 h) |
| Atropine premedication | Not routine; consider for infants or if bradycardia is likely |

## 6. Sedation, analgesia and withdrawal
- Common drips: fentanyl, morphine, midazolam, dexmedetomidine, ketamine.
- After 5 or more days of opioid or benzodiazepine infusions, expect **iatrogenic withdrawal**. Units use a scoring tool (WAT-1) and a wean plan (for example enteral methadone or lorazepam). Pharmacists often write the wean.
- Propofol: long, high dose infusions in children carry risk of propofol infusion syndrome.

## 7. Everyday pediatric dosing anchors
- Acetaminophen 15 mg/kg every 6 hours (max 75 mg/kg/day, not over 4 g/day).
- Ibuprofen 10 mg/kg every 6 to 8 hours (6 months and older; max 40 mg/kg/day).
- Maintenance fluids: Holliday Segar 4/2/1 rule for rate; AAP 2018 recommends isotonic fluids with appropriate KCl and dextrose for most hospitalized children 28 days to 18 years.
- Ondansetron 0.15 mg/kg (max 8 mg per the Canadian Paediatric Society; weight bands 2, 4, 6 to 8 mg); age 6 months or older. Updated 2026-10-17 (was "max 4 mg in most protocols"); see [[Pediatric_Neonatal_Dosing]].

## 8. Pediatric toxicology to know cold
- Acetaminophen: NAC (21 hour IV protocol; know St. Mary's regimen, many sites now use a two bag protocol).
- Iron, tricyclics (bicarbonate), button battery (honey or sucralfate before arrival, per guidance), clonidine, sulfonylureas (octreotide).
- Poison Control: 1 800 222 1222.
