# IV Room and Sterile Compounding: Pharmacist Core (Module 10)

**Pharmacist's job:** nothing leaves the IV room unless the product matches the order, the concentration fits the line, the label is right, and the beyond use date (BUD) fits the USP 797 category.
**Key:** ✅✅ two independent sources · ✅ one source · ⚠️ [VERIFY] · Drug rules: [[IV_Compatibility]]

## 1. USP 797 (2023) categories and BUDs
| Category | Where made | BUD room temp | BUD refrigerated | Frozen | Check |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Category 1 | ISO 5 hood in an unclassified segregated compounding area | 12 h | 24 h | | ✅✅ [1][2] |
| Category 2, aseptic from sterile ingredients, no sterility test | ISO 5 hood in a classified cleanroom suite | 4 days | 10 days | 45 days | ✅✅ [1][2] |
| Category 2, terminally sterilized with sterility testing | Cleanroom | 45 days | 60 days | 90 days | ✅✅ [1][2] |
| Category 3 | Cleanroom plus extra testing and monitoring | Up to 90 days | Up to 120 days | Up to 180 days | ✅ [1] |
| Immediate use | Emergencies only; administration begins within 4 h of the start of preparation; no more than 3 different sterile products; label with ingredients, preparer and the 4 h time unless the preparer gives it | | | | ✅✅ [Florida DOH: USP 797 inspection checklist (2024)](https://www.floridahealth.gov/licensing-and-regulation/enforcement/inspection-program/SterileCompounding-Revision1,USP7972022.August2024.pdf); [Infusion journal: immediate use SOP (2023)](https://pubs.royle.com/article/Developing+An+Immediate-Use+Compounding+SOP/4632352/800549/article.html) |

BUD can never be longer than the drug's own stability allows ✅ [1].

## 2. Rooms and garbing
- ISO 5 = hood (primary engineering control); ISO 7 = buffer room; ISO 8 = ante room (ISO 7 if it opens to a negative pressure buffer room) ✅ [Florida DOH: USP 797 inspection checklist (2024)](https://www.floridahealth.gov/licensing-and-regulation/enforcement/inspection-program/SterileCompounding-Revision1,USP7972022.August2024.pdf).
- First air: never block HEPA air between the filter and the critical site.
- Garb dirty to clean: shoe covers, hair cover, mask, wash hands, gown, sanitize, sterile gloves ⚠️ [VERIFY St. Mary's SOP].
- Hazardous drugs (USP 800): negative pressure room, biological safety cabinet or isolator, two pairs of chemo gloves, closed system transfer devices ⚠️ [VERIFY].

## 3. The product check (every bag, every syringe)
1. Patient, drug, dose, diluent, volume, final concentration against the order.
2. Vials and syringes used match the dose (pull back check).
3. Concentration fits the line: peripheral or central ([[IV_Compatibility]]).
4. Rate math on the label (units/h ÷ units/mL = mL/h).
5. Container, tubing, filter and light protection.
6. BUD matches the USP 797 category and drug stability.
7. Look at the bag: particles, cloudiness, leaks.
8. Peds and neonatal: recheck weight based math and the max dose; watch preservatives.

## 4. Classic IV room catches
- Phenytoin in dextrose, or pushed faster than 50 mg/min.
- Liposomal amphotericin in saline.
- Nitroglycerin in a PVC bag.
- Amiodarone above 2 mg/mL going peripheral.
- PN above 900 mOsm/L going peripheral.
- 0.22 micron filter on a lipid containing PN.
- Vincristine in a syringe.
- 10 fold rate errors (144 vs 14.4 mL/h).
- A Category 1 product labeled with a 48 h refrigerated BUD.
