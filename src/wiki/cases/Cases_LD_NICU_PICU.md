# Practice Cases: L&D, NICU, PICU

All cases are invented for teaching. Answer out loud in under 60 seconds, then check the model answer. Score yourself: Weak / Solid / Top 10%.
Top 10% = right answer + the source and year + the exact recommendation + what to monitor.

---

## Case 1 (L&D): Magnesium at 2 a.m.
A 31 year old at 36 weeks with preeclampsia with severe features is on magnesium sulfate 2 g/h. The nurse calls: urine output has been 15 mL/h for 2 hours, respiratory rate 11, patellar reflexes absent.

**Model answer:** "Stop the magnesium now. This looks like magnesium toxicity: loss of reflexes and a respiratory rate under 12, with low urine output so it's accumulating. Give calcium gluconate 1 g IV over about 3 minutes, support her breathing, and send a STAT magnesium level and creatinine. Call the OB now. When she's stable and the level is back, restart at a lower rate per protocol if she still needs seizure prophylaxis."
Source: ACOG Practice Bulletin 222 (2020). Linked: [[LD_Obstetrics_Core]].

## Case 2 (L&D): Hemorrhage order
Postpartum hemorrhage after vaginal delivery. Oxytocin is running. The OB asks for methylergonovine. The patient's history shows preeclampsia this pregnancy.

**Model answer:** "Methylergonovine is contraindicated with hypertension and preeclampsia. Carboprost 250 mcg IM is the next option if she doesn't have asthma, or misoprostol. Tranexamic acid 1 g IV over 10 minutes is recommended if we're within 3 hours of birth (WOMAN trial). Would you like carboprost and TXA?"

## Case 3 (L&D): GBS and an allergy
GBS positive patient in labor. Allergy: penicillin, "rash as a child." Order: vancomycin.

**Model answer:** "A childhood rash without hives, anaphylaxis or a severe skin reaction is a low risk allergy. ACOG Committee Opinion 797 (2020) recommends cefazolin 2 g IV, then 1 g every 8 hours, for low risk penicillin allergy. Cefazolin counts as adequate prophylaxis for the baby; vancomycin doesn't, and it adds nephrotoxicity risk. Can I change it to cefazolin?"

## Case 4 (NICU): Decimal check
A 1.2 kg preterm infant. Order: gentamicin 12 mg IV every 24 hours.

**Model answer:** "That's 10 mg/kg, about double the usual neonatal dose (around 4 to 5 mg/kg per dose depending on gestational age). It looks like a calculation error; at 4 to 5 mg/kg the dose would be about 4.8 to 6 mg. Preterm infants also often need a longer interval than every 24 hours. I'd hold it and call the prescriber to confirm the dose and interval from our neonatal chart, plus a level plan."

## Case 5 (NICU): Ceftriaxone in a jaundiced newborn
A 4 day old with jaundice and suspected sepsis. Order: ceftriaxone IV, and calcium gluconate is running for hypocalcemia.

**Model answer:** "Two problems. Ceftriaxone displaces bilirubin and should be avoided in hyperbilirubinemic neonates, and in neonates it must never be given with IV calcium because of fatal precipitates (FDA warning). Recommend cefepime or ceftazidime with ampicillin per our neonatal sepsis protocol."

## Case 6 (PICU): Seizure
A 22 kg 6 year old has been seizing for 7 minutes. IV access is in. Lorazepam is ordered.

**Model answer:** "Lorazepam 0.1 mg/kg is 2.2 mg IV (max 4 mg). It can repeat once in 5 minutes. If she's still seizing, second line is levetiracetam 60 mg/kg, which is 1,320 mg IV, or fosphenytoin 20 mg PE/kg, which is 440 mg PE. ESETT showed similar efficacy among levetiracetam, fosphenytoin and valproate."

## Case 7 (PICU): Code doses
A 15 kg child in asystole.

**Model answer:** "Epinephrine 0.15 mg IV, which is 1.5 mL of the 0.1 mg/mL, every 3 to 5 minutes. PALS 2025 says give it as early as possible in nonshockable rhythms. If the rhythm changes to VF: shock 2 J/kg, which is 30 J, then 4 J/kg, and amiodarone 75 mg (5 mg/kg)."

## Case 8 (PICU): DKA
A 30 kg 10 year old in DKA. Order: insulin 3 units IV bolus, then 3 units/h. K is 3.0.

**Model answer:** "Two changes. ISPAD 2022 recommends no insulin bolus in children, because of cerebral edema risk. And with K at 3.0, ISPAD says to start potassium replacement before insulin; many protocols hold insulin until K is at least 3.3 [VERIFY St. Mary's cutoff]. Then start 0.05 to 0.1 units/kg/h, which is 1.5 to 3 units/h. Have mannitol or 3% saline available for cerebral edema."
