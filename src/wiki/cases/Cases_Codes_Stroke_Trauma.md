# Practice Cases: Codes, Stroke, Trauma

All cases are invented for teaching. 60 seconds each, out loud. Top 10% = answer + source and year + exact order + monitoring.

---

## Case 1 (Code): Dialysis patient in PEA
A 64 year old on hemodialysis who missed two sessions is in PEA. Peaked T waves were seen before the arrest.

**Model answer:** "Suspect hyperkalemia. Calcium chloride 1 g IV now, then insulin regular 10 units IV with dextrose 25 g, and sodium bicarbonate 50 mEq if acidotic. Keep epinephrine 1 mg every 3 to 5 minutes going; in nonshockable rhythms AHA 2025 says give it as early as possible. After ROSC, recheck potassium and glucose and plan dialysis."

## Case 2 (Code): Wrong dose risk
A nurse asks for "adenosine 6 mg" for a patient with a heart transplant.

**Model answer:** "Heart transplant patients are very sensitive to adenosine because the heart is denervated. Start with 3 mg. Same for patients on dipyridamole or carbamazepine, or if it's given through a central line."

## Case 3 (Stroke): Tenecteplase
A 78 kg patient, last known well 2 hours ago, NIHSS 9, BP 192/104, on no anticoagulants. The team chooses tenecteplase.

**Model answer:** "BP has to be below 185/110 first: labetalol 10 to 20 mg IV, or start nicardipine 5 mg/h. Tenecteplase stroke dose is 0.25 mg/kg, which is 19.5 mg here, max 25 mg, as a single IV bolus over 5 seconds, using our weight band chart. Not the cardiac dose. Then BP below 180/105 for 24 hours and no antithrombotics for 24 hours. AHA/ASA 2026 lists tenecteplase as equally preferred to alteplase."

## Case 4 (Stroke): ICH on apixaban
An 82 year old on apixaban (last dose 4 hours ago) has an intracerebral hemorrhage. SBP 196.

**Model answer:** "Reverse with 4F-PCC 50 units/kg per our protocol; andexanet was withdrawn from the US market in 2025. Lower SBP to a target of 140, staying within 130 to 150 and avoiding drops below 130 (AHA/ASA 2022), with a nicardipine or clevidipine drip. Hold apixaban."

## Case 5 (Stroke): SAH
A patient with aneurysmal SAH has an order for nimodipine 60 mg IV every 4 hours.

**Model answer:** "Nimodipine is oral or by feeding tube only, never IV; there's an FDA boxed warning because injecting capsule contents has caused deaths. The order should be 60 mg by mouth or tube every 4 hours for 21 days (AHA/ASA 2023). If her BP drops, 30 mg every 2 hours."

## Case 6 (Trauma): TXA timing
A motorcycle crash patient arrives 4 hours after injury with ongoing bleeding. The resident orders TXA.

**Model answer:** "CRASH-2 found TXA works best given early and may cause harm when started more than 3 hours after injury. At 4 hours it isn't recommended. Let's focus on balanced transfusion with calcium replacement guided by ionized calcium. Did EMS already give TXA?"

## Case 7 (Trauma): Open fracture
A 130 kg patient with a type III open tibia fracture, no allergies.

**Model answer:** "Antibiotics within the hour (EAST). Cefazolin 3 g IV, since he's over 120 kg, plus gram negative coverage for a type III per our order set. Check tetanus: if his history is unknown, Tdap plus tetanus immune globulin 250 units IM."

## Case 8 (Trauma): RSI choice
A trauma patient with 40% burns 3 days ago needs reintubation. Succinylcholine is ordered.

**Model answer:** "Avoid succinylcholine. After 24 to 48 hours, burn patients can have severe hyperkalemia from succinylcholine. Rocuronium 1 to 1.2 mg/kg is the alternative, with sugammadex available for rescue."
